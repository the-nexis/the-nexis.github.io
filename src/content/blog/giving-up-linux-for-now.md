I absolutely LOVE Linux. Really. I started experimenting with different distributions about two years ago. After the initial distro hopping, I settled on Mint, which was really nice as a beginner's distro, and I learned a lot. Then I tried Pop!_OS because I liked the COSMIC desktop environment, but ended up disliking the feel of it. Eventually, I switched to Arch, which I've been using for about a year now. It's been awesome.

I used [HyprFlux](https://github.com/ahmad9059/HyprFlux) together with CachyOS packages and the CachyOS kernel. I can't recommend it enough, especially if you code. The Hyprland layout just clicked for me.

So, why am I leaving it behind? Well, the way I code and work has changed a lot. With agentic coding, I spend more time waiting, and Windows makes it easier for me to use that time for entertainment, creative work, or studying. Because of that, I started some projects on the Windows system that was designated for "fun" instead of the Linux system designated for "work" (I used to dual-boot my laptop). It pains me to say I spent almost the whole summer booting up Arch only a few times.

Things like Azure tools and Teams, which I need for work, also run more smoothly on Windows, at least in my experience. Keeping my browser in sync between the two systems got annoying as well.

What comes next, then? Am I giving up on FOSS and becoming a corporate rat? While I can't speak to the latter, the former is definitely not the case. I still plan on using Arch inside WSL for coding. I'm way more comfortable with a Linux shell than with PowerShell. I just won't have a dedicated Linux system on my laptop, at least for the foreseeable future.

Is it a good idea? Who knows, really. Am I gonna regret it? Possibly. Stay tuned.

**Per Audacia Ad Astra!**
